VA Lead Pool

Unclaimed opportunities — county disabled-veteran tax exemptions (DVHS / DV1–4) plus clues of an active mortgage. Claim a row to add it to My Leads and start tracking. Read the Why call column first.

What this page is for

Each row is a Bexar homeowner the county already marked with a disabled-veteran tax exemption (DVHS / DV1–4). We then look for public clues they still have a mortgage — usually a lender paying their taxes or a Deed of Trust. The Why call column turns that into a plain sentence so you know whether to dial, research, or skip.

  • Hot — call now: veteran signal + fresh mortgage evidence.
  • Warm — likely worth a soft check; mortgage clue is thinner or incomplete.
  • Watch — do not pitch a refinance yet; we only know they’re DV*, not that they owe a loan.

Why these people are often better off with a VA loan

County DV* status does not mean they already have a VA mortgage. Many still carry a conventional / FHA / portfolio loan. If they qualify for VA entitlement, a VA loan can beat that loan on cost — especially for DVHS / high disability owners who are often funding-fee exempt.

No monthly PMI Conventional loans under ~20% equity charge PMI. VA loans do not — so payment can drop even at a similar rate.
Funding-fee exemption Many disabled veterans pay $0 VA funding fee. That removes a big upfront cost conventional / FHA closing costs or PMI never erase.
Often lower rate / payment VA rates are frequently competitive with conventional. Pair that with no PMI and the monthly savings can be material.
Flexible equity / cash-out VA purchase, cash-out, or IRRRL (if already VA) can refinance high-rate debt or pull equity without a conventional PMI restart.

Honest limit: public records cannot prove their rate, balance, or VA entitlement. The call is a review — if they are already on a strong VA loan or paid off, you walk away. Hot leads are people where county + lender clues say that review is worth their time.

Sample homeowner approach (phone / door)

Keep it public-record honest. Do not cite disability rating or imply access to VA medical data.

Hi [Name] — this is [Your name] with [Company]. I help Bexar County homeowners who already have a disabled-veteran property-tax exemption on the appraisal roll.

I'm not calling from the VA or the tax office. County records show a veteran homestead exemption on [street], and there may still be a mortgage or Deed of Trust on the property. A lot of veterans in that situation are on a conventional loan and haven't checked whether a VA loan — often with no PMI and, for many disabled veterans, no funding fee — would lower the payment.

Would you be open to a short, no-obligation numbers check of your current loan versus VA options? If you're already on a great VA rate or paid off, I'll get out of your hair. If not, I can tell you plainly whether a refinance is even worth doing.
